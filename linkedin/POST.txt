The same answerability gate scored 100% and 69.5% on the same afternoon.

Same model, same prompt, 200 items per slice. The only thing that changed was how I built the wrong answers.

I was testing a cheap pre-check for RAG: before spending a generation call, ask whether the retrieved passage actually contains what the question needs. The judge was Jev, TypeSafe AI's System One model, which returns a typed score instead of prose.

Two kinds of wrong answer:

Random unrelated passage: 200/200 correct.
On topic but missing the fact: 139/200.

The second is what a retrieval miss really looks like. Nothing hands you a passage about jellyfish when you asked about Warsaw. It hands you the right article, missing the one sentence you needed. On those, 61 of 200 were called answerable when they were not.

The 100% is not the good news it looks like either. A classifier that always says "unanswerable" also scores 100% there, and 0% on real answers.

More useful than the accuracy was where the uncertainty landed. Across the 400 answerable and hard-negative items, at a band of 0.10 to 0.90:

At or below 0.10, drop the passage and retrieve again: 14.5%.
Between 0.10 and 0.90, escalate the judgement: 42.5%.
At or above 0.90, pass through to generation: 43%.

Two things in that split are worth more than the accuracy figure.

133 of the 200 hard negatives fell in the uncertain band. The gate mostly declined to commit on the class it is worst at, which is the behaviour you want from a router.

The errors run both ways, unevenly. One answerable passage was dropped at the low end. Ten unanswerable ones were waved through at 0.90 or above, and that is the direction that produces a confident answer built on a passage that never held the fact.

On cost, carefully: rejecting a passage avoids the generation call outright. An escalated judgement that comes back negative would too, but I did not measure that second path, so there is no end-to-end RAG saving here. The gate decides whether to generate, not what to generate.

Limits. 600 decisions, one English dataset. I chose 0.10 and 0.90 by reading this same data, so every split above is in-sample and says nothing about production reliability. High-confidence scores were dependable on this evaluation and mid-range ones were not, which is measured, not validated. SQuAD 2.0 is public, so contamination is possible and I have not ruled it out.
