The same answerability gate scored 100% and 69.5% on the same afternoon.

Same model. Same prompt. The only thing that changed was how I built the negative examples.

I was testing Jev, TypeSafe AI's System One model, as a cheap pre-check for RAG: can this retrieved passage actually answer the question?

📊 Two kinds of negative:

🟢 Random unrelated passage: 100% (200/200)

🟠 On topic, missing the fact: 69.5% (139/200)

A 30.5-point gap, and the second number is the one that counts. A retrieval miss doesn't hand you something obviously irrelevant. It hands you the right article, missing the one sentence you needed.

That 100% is worth less than it looks, too. A classifier that always says "unanswerable" scores 100% there and 0% on real answers.

🔍 Then I read the 61 cases it got wrong. That changed the finding.

Of the 10 it was most confident about, at least 6 were not wrong at all.

"How many stories does the Bank of America tower have?" The passage says the tower "includes 42 floors." SQuAD labels that unanswerable. It isn't.

So 69.5% is a floor, not an estimate. Some of the gap is the benchmark's error, not the model's.

⚠️ But the real mistakes had a shape, and it's a useful one.

Most of the remaining failures were one thing: a question subtly altered against the passage. SQuAD builds its unanswerable questions by perturbing answerable ones, and that is what the gate missed:

• Name swap: "Carl von Hampson and William Linde" when the passage says Linde and Hampson.
• Negation: "What IS fully understood about γδ T cells?" when the passage says not fully understood.
• False premise: asking what was gained from a win that the passage describes as a defeat.

It handles topic mismatch almost perfectly. It misses a question that has been subtly altered against the passage.

Limits: 600 decisions, one public English dataset, thresholds picked on this same data, and I re-read those 61 myself knowing what the model had said. That is a re-reading, not an independent re-labelling.

My takeaway: your benchmark can be wrong in both directions. Convenient negatives flatter the model. Noisy labels punish it. I only found both by reading the failures one at a time.
